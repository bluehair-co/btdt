About BT•DT

BT•DT (been there, done that) turns your Google Timeline into a map of everywhere you've been, a timeline of every trip, and insights into how you get around and the CO₂ it costs.

Your data never leaves your device

Your files are read in this browser and kept only here. There is no server, no account and no tracking, so nobody, me included, ever sees your files. Clearing the app's data in your browser removes it completely.

What you can load
  • Timeline.json exported from Google Maps on an Android phone (the current format).
  • Timeline export from an iPhone (Google Maps for iOS).
  • Google Takeout files from before Timeline moved to phones: Records.json (raw GPS points) and Semantic Location History (one file per month). Load them together with Timeline.json and the app uses the best of each.
  • Strava: the account export ZIP, or single .gpx, .tcx or .fit files. Load them from Settings → Load Strava export once your Timeline is in. Each activity replaces the Timeline trip it covers and keeps its sport.

Flights don't need a file of their own: they come from Timeline, and long jumps Timeline missed are recognised as flights too. You can load several files at once, or a whole folder.

How to export your data

Android

  1. Open Settings → Location → Location services → Timeline.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data and save Timeline.json.

iPhone

  1. In Google Maps, tap your profile picture, then Settings → Personal content.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data and save the file.

Older Google Takeout

If you downloaded Location History from takeout.google.com before Google moved Timeline onto phones, load Records.json and the files in Semantic Location History. Takeout no longer offers new ones.

Strava

  1. On strava.com open Settings → My Account → Download or Delete Your Account and request your archive.
  2. Strava emails a ZIP. Load it as it is; only the activity files inside are read, so photos don't slow it down.
How it works
  • Saved in your browser. Your last import is kept in this browser's storage, so the app opens with it next time.
  • Trips are cleaned up. Bad GPS jumps, stale start and end points, time parked counted as driving and places Google moved are fixed, and loose GPS points become trips. Fixed trips are flagged in their details.
  • Local times and countries come from time zone data and country borders built into the app, so they work offline.
  • CO₂ is estimated per trip from its distance and transport, with the UK government's 2025 averages.
  • The only things fetched from the internet are the map tiles (Esri) and the map library. Add the app to your home screen and it opens offline.
