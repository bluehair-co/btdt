About BT•DT

BT•DT (been there, done that) turns your location history into a map of everywhere you've been, a timeline of every trip, and insights into how you get around and the CO₂ it costs. Start with your Google Timeline, add the activities from your watch or bike computer, or load any GPS track.

Your data never leaves your device

Your files are read in this browser and kept only here. There is no server, no account and no tracking, so nobody, me included, ever sees your files. Clearing the app's data in your browser removes it completely.

How it works
  • Saved in your browser. Your last import is kept in this browser's storage, so the app opens with it next time.
  • Trips are cleaned up. Bad GPS jumps, stale start and end points, time parked counted as driving and places Google moved are fixed, and loose GPS points become trips. Fixed trips are flagged in their details.
  • Local times and countries come from time zone data and country borders built into the app, so they work offline.
  • CO₂ is estimated per trip from its distance and transport, with the UK government's 2025 averages.
  • The only things fetched from the internet are the map tiles (Esri), the map and globe libraries, the fonts, and the station packs you choose. Add the app to your home screen and it opens offline.
  • Place names are only looked up when you press Get the name from OpenStreetMap while naming a place: that place's location goes to OpenStreetMap (Nominatim and Overpass), nothing else.
  • Public transport lines for a routine come only from your station packs when you have one for that country (never online there). Elsewhere, and for buses and ferries, they are only looked up when you press Use the line from OpenStreetMap while editing it: the routine's two ends go to OpenStreetMap (Overpass), nothing else.
  • Station packs (railway stations, tram and metro stops and lines, per country) are downloaded from GitHub only when you choose a country, after an import or in Settings. Only the country you chose goes to GitHub; the pack stays on your device and works offline.
  • Trips Timeline merged, such as a train ride and the tram after it kept as one trip, are split into their legs where the GPS points show a stop or a change of speed. Each leg shows the trip it came from, and Keep as one trip undoes it.

Download station packs?

A station pack holds a country's railway stations, tram and metro stops and lines, from OpenStreetMap. With it, BT•DT can tell where Timeline merged a train ride and the tram after it into one trip, name the station you changed at and the stations and stops among your regular places, and draw a routine's line along the railway, all offline. Downloading one tells GitHub, where the packs are kept, which country you chose and nothing about your trips. You can add or remove packs in Settings any time.

How to get your files

Your data never leaves your device

Your files are read in this browser and kept only here. There is no server, no account and no tracking, so nobody, me included, ever sees your files. Clearing the app's data in your browser removes it completely.

What you can load
  • Timeline.json exported from Google Maps on an Android phone (the current format).
  • Timeline export from an iPhone (Google Maps for iOS).
  • Google Takeout files from before Timeline moved to phones: Records.json (raw GPS points) and Semantic Location History (one file per month). Load them together with Timeline.json and the app uses the best of each.
  • Dawarich: an export from the Points page (GeoJSON or GPX, as the ZIP Dawarich gives you or unzipped), or the whole account export from Export my data. Trips and stays are worked out from its GPS points, with your visits' names and Dawarich's transport types when the account export has them.
  • Strava: the account export ZIP. Each activity replaces the Timeline trip it covers and keeps its sport, name and gear.
  • Garmin Connect: the account export ZIP, or single .fit, .gpx or .tcx activities. Fitbit, Polar, Suunto, Coros and Wahoo activities load the same way. Each activity keeps its sport and its full GPS track, and replaces the Timeline trip it covers.
  • Any GPS track: .gpx from Komoot, OsmAnd, GPSLogger and most apps, .kml or .kmz from Google Earth, and .geojson. A track of one journey is a trip; a log of whole days (a tracker that records everything) has its trips and stays worked out from its points, as with Dawarich.

Flights don't need a file of their own: they come from Timeline, and long jumps Timeline missed are recognised as flights too. You can load several files at once, or a whole folder.

How to export your data

Android

  1. Open Settings → Location → Location services → Timeline.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data and save Timeline.json.

iPhone

  1. In Google Maps, tap your profile picture, then Settings → Personal content.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data and save the file.

Older Google Takeout

If you downloaded Location History from takeout.google.com before Google moved Timeline onto phones, load Records.json and the files in Semantic Location History. Takeout no longer offers new ones.

Dawarich

  1. On the Points page, pick the dates and choose Export as GeoJSON (or GPX). Download the ZIP from the Exports page and load it as it is.
  2. Or, for everything with your visits and transport types, use Export my data in Dawarich's settings and load that ZIP.

Strava

  1. On strava.com open Settings → My Account → Download or Delete Your Account and request your archive.
  2. Strava emails a ZIP. Load it as it is; only the activity files inside are read, so photos don't slow it down.

Garmin Connect

  1. For everything: request your data at garmin.com → Account → Data Management → Export Your Data. Garmin emails a ZIP; load it as it is.
  2. For one activity: open it on connect.garmin.com, choose the gear menu and Export Original (a .fit file, in a ZIP) or Export to GPX.

Fitbit

  1. On fitbit.com open an activity with GPS, choose the menu and Export as TCX File. Load the .tcx files, or several in a ZIP.

Other apps

Komoot, Polar Flow, Suunto, Coros, Wahoo, OsmAnd and most trackers can export an activity or a track as GPX. Only recorded tracks have times; planned routes have none, so they can't be placed on your timeline.

The map cut into squares of about 1.5 km (zoom 14 map tiles). A square counts once any trip on the ground passed through it. Your gear

The bikes and shoes your Strava activities were recorded with, and how far each went. Tap one to see only its trips.

Heart rate

From the activities in the filters that recorded heart rate. Fitness: your heart rate each month while going at about the same speed (within 8% of it), leaving out each activity's first 5 minutes while your heart rate settles. The same speed at a lower heart rate means you got fitter; heat, hills, wind and tiredness push it up too, so look at the trend over several months. Zones are shares of your maximum heart rate, which you can set in Settings.

Select trips

Draw around an area to pick every trip on the map that passes through it, then change their type at once. Filter first to narrow down what can be picked, such as In vehicle only.

Map snaps

Draw your trips between two stations along one railway, tram or metro line of your station packs, to clean up their paths. Tap the two stations on the map, pick the way, and choose how closely a trip has to follow it.
