# BT•DT (Been There · Done That)

BT•DT 2.0 shows where you've been and how you get around, using your Google Timeline. It is the successor to Transport Footprint, which is frozen as v1.0 in `../versions/v1.0/`.

## Open it

- **On a Mac or PC:** open `dist/index.html` in a browser.
- **On a phone:** host the `dist/` folder over HTTPS (for example GitHub Pages or Netlify), open it, and choose *Add to Home screen*. It then starts offline, and your last import stays saved on the phone.

Your Timeline file is read on the device and is never uploaded.

## Strava

In Imported data (at the top of Filters), *Import more files* also takes the ZIP from Strava (Settings → My Account → Download or Delete Your Account), or single .gpx, .fit or .tcx files. Only the activity files inside the ZIP are read, so a large export with photos is fine. A Strava activity replaces every Timeline trip that is at least half inside its time (whatever transport Timeline guessed), and activities Timeline missed are added. The replaced Timeline trips are only hidden while Strava is shown: choosing Timeline as the only source in Filters brings them back. Virtual rides are skipped. The Strava activities are saved apart from the Timeline trips, so re-importing either one keeps the match. Each Strava trip keeps its sport (Kayaking, Mountain bike, Trail run…) under the matching transport type, which is what the trip is called in the app; the Transport filter lists the sports present under their type. Data loaded before sports were read shows them after loading the Strava export again.

An activity's name from Strava is the trip's title (in the Timeline list and the trip details), with its sport under it, and its description is shown in the trip details. The gear it was recorded with (activities.csv's *Activity Gear*) is listed in the trip details, and in Filters each bike or pair of shoes is a chip under the transport type it was mostly used for (bikes under Cycling, below its Strava sports), with *No gear* for that type's other trips; ticking or unticking the type does its gear too. Ticking a gear on also ticks the Strava sports (or types) its trips are under, so its trips show even after *None*, leaving the type partly on; unticking it unticks those that no other ticked gear needs. Ticking a sport on when none of the gear its trips use is ticked ticks that gear. During playback, a ride on a Halfbike, Strida or cargo bike moves with that bike's icon. The export's bikes.csv and shoes.csv say which gear is a bike and which are shoes; a bike whose name, brand or model says Halfbike (or Kolelinia) gets the Halfbike icon, one that says Strida the Strida icon, and one that names a front-loading cargo bike (Urban Arrow, Babboe, Bullitt, bakfiets, cargo…) the cargo bike icon, also on its trips. Without those files, gear used mostly for cycling is a bike and gear used on foot is shoes. Insights has a *Your gear* card with the distance, activities and dates of each gear in the trips shown; tapping one filters to its trips. Data loaded before names and gear were kept shows them from the activities.csv columns it kept, or after loading the export again.

During a day's playback (a single day chosen, not a week, month or year), while a trip is under way, a pill under the moving marker shows the speed at that moment (`posSpeed`): from the trip's speed series (`tripSpeeds`, as in the trip chart), interpolated, or the trip's average speed when it has none (flights, trips of a few points), in km/h or mph as in Settings. It hides between trips and while the trip stands still (under 1 km/h). The globe and other projections draw the same pill on their canvas.

Cycling uses Material's pedal_bike icon. A trip whose sport the data names gets that sport's Material icon when there is one: electric_bike for an e-bike or e-mountain bike ride, accessible_forward for a handcycle (other rides keep pedal_bike, as Material has no mountain bike or gravel icon). Gear with an icon of its own (Halfbike, Strida, cargo bike) wins over the sport. The icon shows on the trip's badge in the lists and details, on the Timeline map card, and on the playback and chart markers.

The trip details chart the trip's speed over time, and for a Strava activity whose file recorded it (GPX `<gpxtpx:hr>`, TCX `HeartRateBpm`, FIT record field 3) its heart rate too, on the same time axis (speed on the left scale, bpm on the right). A Strava activity's speed comes from every point in its file over the 20 seconds around each moment, other trips' from their saved line over stretches of at least 20 seconds; both series keep one value every 5 seconds or more (at most 1,500), and the speed scale stops at the 98th percentile so one GPS spike doesn't flatten the rest. Pointing at the chart, or tapping it, shows the time, speed and heart rate there, and puts the trip's transport icon (or gear icon) on the map where it was then; a tap pans the map to it when it is out of view. Strava data loaded before heart rate was read has none until the export is loaded again. The readout has its own line above the chart, so the chart stays put while you point at it. On a wide screen (960 px and up) the button at the chart's top right opens it as a panel along the bottom of the screen; the map, the dock and the side panels move up above it. Dragging the bar at its top (or the arrow keys on it) makes it taller or lower, between 150 px and 70% of the window, and the chart redraws to fill it. While it is open the chart leaves the trip details, and it comes back there when the panel closes. The panel follows the trip that's open and closes with the trip details. On a phone the same button, on the Map tab, opens the chart along the bottom of the screen at up to 40% of its height, and the dock, the top bar, the map buttons and the trip details make way so the trip's map and its chart show together, with the trip fitted into the map above. A tap on an empty spot of the map leaves it open, a tap on another trip switches the chart to it, and a tap on several trips closes it to list them. Closing it brings everything back, trip details included.

## MyRules

A MyRules file (`*.myrules.json`, format in `../docs/myrules.md`, schema in `static/schema/myrules-0.2.json`; 0.1 stays in `myrules-0.1.json`) holds what only the user knows. It loads with the same *Load location files* button, on its own or with location files; a `.json` is taken as MyRules by its name or by a `"myrules"` key at its start (`mrIsFile`). It is kept in IndexedDB next to the trips (key `myrules`) with the list of changes made in the app since it was last loaded or exported. Loading a second file asks to merge (same `id` replaced) or replace. `src/rules.js` has all of it.

- **Gear periods** (`gear`) give trips of their type with no recorded gear that gear, inside their dates, weekdays and time window and their `match` (`min_km`, `max_km`, `sports`, `from`/`to` places, `weekdays_only`). Recorded gear (Strava, activity files) is never replaced. Of the periods that fit, one with a `match` beats one without, then the shorter period wins; a tie leaves the trip without gear and the rules sheet says how many. `strava_name` makes a period share Strava's gear chip.
- **Corrections** (`corrections`) set a trip's type (`mode`), title (`name`) or gear, or hide it (`hide`). They run first in `setData` (`mrApplyCorrections`), on D's own copies of `mode`, `est`, `sport` and `stype` (the files' values stay in `D.base` and in RAW, so nothing saved changes), before anything counts types or builds gear. Corrections on a `day` or `span` apply before those on one trip (`start`), each in file order, so the trip's own and then the newest win. A trip's `start` matches the trip starting within 3 minutes of it (the type in the files picks between two). A new type clears *guessed from speed* and a Strava sport of another type. Gear from a correction on one trip replaces recorded gear; on a day or span it only fills trips without any. Hidden trips are left out in `apply`, so out of the map, lists, totals and exports. `replace` is kept but not applied yet.
- **Routines** (`routines`, `mrApplyRoutines`, run inside `mrApplyCorrections` before the corrections) match trips by their ends (`from`/`to`: `@place` or `[lat, lng]`, within 500 m or the routine's `radius_m`, one number or `{from, to}` per end (`mrEndM`), or the place's radius) and `when`, and back with `both_ways` (`return_when`). Of the routines a trip fits, the one whose line it follows most closely wins (`mrLineScore`; no line counts as 0), then the shorter `when`, then the later rule; one whose line it doesn't follow closely enough can't take it, so the next gets a chance (lines are learned from all the trips that fit each routine). A routine takes a trip only when all its `then` can apply (all or nothing): `mrTypeFits` (the files' type is the same, missing, generic `VEHICLE` (`MR_OPEN`) or guessed, or `force`), its gear fits (`mrGearFits`: no recorded gear, or the same by name), then its line fits. A trip left out keeps everything of its own and `D.mrMiss[i]` says why (`type`, `gear`, `fit`, `noline`, `missing`); the trip details show it as *Not in routine* with *Edit routine*, and the rules sheet counts them (`MR_LEFT`). `then.mode` sets the type on the trips it takes; `then.gear` fills gearless trips before gear periods (`mrApplyGear`). `then.snap` draws its trips along one line: learned (the best trip of the most-taken way in `mrWays`, as *Edit routine* recommends: by `mrClean` (GPS points per km, no gap over 2 min, activities and Records.json lines count double), else by most points, 5 or more), `{ trip }`, `{ polyline }` or `{ geojson }` (`MR.lines`, from a `.geojson` named by a routine and loaded with or after the rules; `importFiles` keeps it out of the location files). Each trip takes the stretch between the points nearest its ends, its times from its own GPS points placed along the line (`mrSnapTimes`: each point within 500 m at how far along it is, forward only, the time between them spread by distance; with none, an even speed), and its length (`D.snap[i]`, `D.km`; the files' km stay in `D.base.km`). A trip with under `then.match` percent (`MR_MATCH`, 67) of its points within `MR_MATCH_KM` (500 m) of the line (`mrLineScore`), or with an end over 1 km off it, isn't snapped and stays out of the routine, unless it is in the routine's `include` (*Add to routine* in its details, `mrIncluded`). *Use the line from OpenStreetMap* (`mrOsmLines`, public transport types in `MR_OSM_ROUTES`) asks Overpass for route relations near both ends, joins their ways (`mrOsmJoin`) and saves the cut as `snap: { polyline, osm }`. The routine dialog has days and a time window (`mrDaysRow`), a match slider with ghosts above it in colour and below it dashed, and tapping a ghost makes it the line. The rules sheet has a tab per kind of rule (`MR_TAB`). `tripPath`, `trail` and the heatmap use the line; the trip details draw the own GPS points dashed under it. *Same on other days?* (`mrOfferRoutine`) follows a type change in *Edit trip* when 5+ like trips exist.
- **Turn off for these trips.** The last section of Filters (`mrHereSec`, drawn by `mrHereLine` after each `apply`) says how many gear periods and routines changed the trips shown (`mrHere`: a gear period's gear in `D.mrGear`, a routine's match in `D.mrRoute`). *Show rules* opens *Rules in these trips* (`mrHereSheet`), each rule with its count and *Turn off for these trips* (`mrTurnOffHere`). *Trips like these* writes the date, year, month, weekday, hour and country filters (`mrFilterCond`) as an entry in the rule's `unless`; *Just these trips* adds each trip shown to its `except`. `mrExcept` leaves out trips matching any `unless` entry (`mrUnless`, `mrUnlessOk`: every part of the entry must fit; countries count at either end of the trip), so gear periods, routines and corrections all skip them. The rules sheet lists each entry under its rule (`mrUnlessText`, "Not on weekends") with *Undo*. A file that gets an `unless` is saved as version 0.2 (`mrUseV2`).
- **Re-applying** after a change (`mrReapply`) keeps the filters: types and sports that now have trips for the first time are turned on, gear chips are rebuilt keeping the ticked ones by name (No gear by its type), with new gear on, so a gear filter still holds after a rule changes which trips have that gear.
- **Places** (`places`) name the stays within their radius, `role: "home"` replaces the home guess (the latest such rule), and `hide: true` leaves the stays there out of the map pins, heatmap and Insights places, and shows trips to and from it by town.
- **Inferred and Edited.** Gear a period filled in shows an *Inferred* tag in the lists and on the trip's gear, the trip details say which rule set it with *Not this trip* (added to the rule's `except`), Raw data and the CSV list it (`gear from MyRules`), and the *Guessed* filter in Filters covers it too. Anything a correction set shows *Edited* instead (flag `edited in MyRules`), with *Undo* per change in the trip details (it drops that action from a one-trip correction, or adds the trip to a day or span correction's `except`); Raw data lists each change with its value, what it was and the rule. Edited trips are not in the *Guessed* filter.
- **Naming a place.** A regular place's page (tap its pin) has *Name this place*: a name, what it is (Home, Work, School, Family, Sport or just a place), the radius that counts as this place (25 m to 1 km), and Hide. *Get the name from OpenStreetMap* (`osmNameFill`, only when tapped, no key) sends the place's centre to Nominatim (the name of what's right there) and Overpass (named places within the radius, up to 300 m); the first name fills the Name field and the others are chips to pick instead, nearest first. Nothing is saved until *Save*, so the name and radius can still be changed. *Define radius* opens a full-screen map of just that place: the radius, the stays within 1.5 km as dots (teal inside, grey outside) with a count, a slider and *Save radius*. It saves a place rule centred on the middle of that spot's stays; *Edit this place* changes it and *Remove the name* deletes it. A named place's radius overrides the app's own grouping. It is tested against the places in the original data (`mrUnits`: each Google place ID at the middle of its stays, and each stay without an ID on its own), so each is taken or left whole, whatever the 300 m places, the 100 m ID join or the merged IDs did with it. Those stays are one spot (`buildSpotKeys` gives it the key `mr:<id>`, placed at the rule's centre) and one place in `tagPlaces` (`mr` set; trip ends within the radius join it too), which takes nothing from the 300 m grouping around it. So pins inside merge into one pin and one place page, and trips to it say its name; removing the name splits them again. *Define radius* shows those original places as dots sized by visits, with how many are inside. Spots just outside a named place keep their town's name. Named places' pins are teal (home stays orange). *What it is* also offers Restaurant, Bar / café, Supermarket, Transport stop and Airport (`role`: `restaurant`, `cafe`, `supermarket`, `transit`, `airport`); a place's kind gives its pin and list badge an icon (`MR_KIND`). Insights' *Time by kind of place* (`renderKinds`) shows once 5 or more places have a kind: hours stayed per kind in the filters, places and share, and everywhere else. *Where you spent time* (`renderPlaces`) ranks by Time or Visits (`PREFS.placesM`) and lists spots (as the pins), not labels, so unnamed spots in one town are separate rows saying where in it they are (`spotHint`: distance and direction from the town centre). Home, Work and School can be *Current* or *Past* (past writes `when.to`, prefilled with the last stay there, always before today): current ones get the sunrise colour (orange, like home) and past ones teal, each with its own icon (home, briefcase, graduation cap), and a group of pins with a current one in it is orange too. A past home is called *Former home* unless it has a name, and isn't home any more: with only past homes in MyRules, home is guessed from the nights again, leaving out places MyRules gives another role or marks as a past home.
- **In the app.** Imported data has a MyRules card (switch for all rules, Open rules, Export). The rules sheet switches or deletes each rule, and tapping one edits it in a dialog over the list (`mrEditRule`): places in their name dialog, a trip fix in *Edit trip*, gear periods (name, kind, period, max km) and routines (name, type, period, both ways, always) in their own; the list is drawn again on save. Routine dialogs (*Same on other days?* and *Edit routine*) have a map of the trips the routine would cover (on a wide screen with the map tab open, `mrAsk({ side })` opens them as a non-modal panel on the right and `mrGhostStart` draws on the main map in its own `mrg` pane over a dimmed layer, `onMapTap` stands down while `mrSideOpen()`, and closing restores the view; otherwise a small map in the dialog) as faint ghost lines (`mrRoutineTrips`, `mrGhostStart`), with how many would change type, redrawn as the form changes. The snap line in use is drawn solid on top; for a learned one, arrows go through the ways its trips take (`mrWays`: within 150 m for 85% of their length, both ways round; most-taken first, each by its cleanest trip), with *Back to the recommended line*, and the chosen one is saved as `snap: { trip }` (`mrGhostSnap`; the recommended one stays `learn`). `mrClean` uses the files' km, so a snapped trip's line length doesn't change its score. Places show the icon of their kind (`mrLook`, `spotLook`) on pins, in Where you spent time, Spots here, nearby spots and on their page. *Edit trip* in any trip's details sets its type, title and Hide, written into that trip's correction (one per trip, created or updated; emptied, it is removed). *Set gear* in a ride's, run's or walk's details writes the gear into that correction (just this trip) or a gear period (dates prefilled from the gear's first and last trip, with a live count). Each correction in the rules sheet lists what it does, with *Open trip* (hidden trips included).
- **Nothing lost.** Changes not exported show on the card. *Remove from this device* (which now always asks first) and *Replace* first offer to download them; the list of changes is behind *See the N changes*.
- `replace` corrections are read, kept and exported, but not applied yet. The demo ignores your own saved MyRules.

## GPX, KML, GeoJSON, TCX and FIT files

The main Load button also reads location files from other apps and devices: `.gpx` (Garmin, Komoot, Suunto, Polar, Wahoo, OsmAnd, GPSLogger…), `.kml` and `.kmz` (Google Earth tracks `<gx:Track>`, and placemarks with a time span), `.geojson` (lines with `coordTimes`/`coordinateProperties.times` or a Unix time as the 4th number, and points with a time), `.tcx` (Fitbit, Garmin) and `.fit` (Garmin and most fitness devices), gzipped or in a ZIP. A ZIP that isn't Strava's or Dawarich's is searched for these files, one ZIP deep inside it too, which covers Garmin's account export (`DI_CONNECT/…/UploadedFiles_*.zip`).

Each track is either a recorded activity or a log of where you were:

- **TCX and FIT** files are always one activity. Any other track is an activity unless it lasts over 16 hours, stops recording for over 90 minutes, or stays within 150 m for over 90 minutes.
- **Activities** are handled exactly like Strava's: they keep their full track, speed and heart rate for the trip chart, their sport (GPX `<type>`, TCX `Sport`, FIT `sport`/`sub_sport`) and name, replace the Timeline trips they cover, and are listed under the Source filter (called *Activities*, or the app's name when they all come from one app). The app or device that wrote the file is kept (GPX `creator`, TCX `Creator`, FIT `file_id.manufacturer`) and shown on the trip; Strava's keep Strava's mark, others a small track mark.
- **Logs** (and timed points) are rebuilt into trips and stays like a Dawarich export (source *GPS tracks*), and work like Records.json next to a Google Timeline.
- Tracks without times (planned routes and courses) are left out, with a message saying so.

Speed-based guessing of a log's transport joins legs shorter than a minute before measuring their speed, so dense recordings (a point every few seconds) aren't all read as walking.

## Dawarich

[Dawarich](https://dawarich.app) is a self-hosted location history app. Its exports load with the same *Load Timeline file* button, and are recognised by what is inside them:

- **Export as GeoJSON or GPX** (Points page): a ZIP holding one file, GeoJSON with a point feature per GPS point (time in `properties.timestamp`) or GPX written by Dawarich (one track with every point). The file unzipped works too.
- **Export my data** (Settings): a ZIP with `manifest.json` and the points as JSON Lines in `points/YYYY/YYYY-MM.jsonl`, plus `visits/` and `tracks/`. A month of points unzipped (`.jsonl`) loads on its own too.

Dawarich keeps raw GPS points, so trips and stays are worked out from them, all read as a stream so a large export never has to fit in memory as text. Points less accurate than 200 m are left out. A stay is points that keep within 150 m of their middle for at least 10 minutes (a phone that stops reporting while still counts, when its next point is there too); stays at the same spot less than 15 minutes apart are one. The points between two stays are a trip when they get 500 m from where they started or end 200 m from it, starting and ending at the stays' middles; a run ends at a gap of over 30 minutes, and a jump of over 150 km at over 180 km/h is a flight, as in Records.json. The transport is the one on Dawarich's track segments when the account export has them (low-confidence ones are ignored), and a trip is split where it changes; otherwise it is guessed from the speed, as for loose Timeline points, and marked as guessed. A stay takes the name of the Dawarich visit covering most of its time (or most of the visit's) within 500 m, and that visit's place position when it is within 300 m; declined and deleted visits are left out. Times are UTC in Dawarich, so they take the time zone of the place, like Records.json.

Loaded together with a Google Timeline, Dawarich works like Records.json: Timeline wins the days it covers, Dawarich trips are kept in the gaps it left, and Timeline trips are drawn along Dawarich's GPS points.

## Trips from Timeline's loose GPS points

Older on-device Timeline data often keeps GPS points while you were moving but no trip for them, so a day of driving shows up as a few stays and nothing else. Points outside every trip and stay are joined into trips (a run ends at a gap of over 30 minutes, a jump of over 50 km, or a trip or stay) when they get at least 500 m from where they started. Their transport type is guessed from the speed they kept while moving, weighted by distance (the speed at or above which half the distance was covered, and never less than the trip's overall average, so one long fast stretch between a few slow legs isn't a bike ride): 30 km/h or more is a car, 10 km/h or more a bike, slower is walking. A run with at least 2 km (and a fifth of its distance) at over 45 km/h is a car too, however slow the rest, since no bike keeps that up; that is often a ride to the station and the train after it, kept as one run, and the train checks below can then make it a train. They are marked as guessed, and the trip details say they were made from loose points.

## Trips Timeline merged

Timeline sometimes keeps a train ride, the wait at the station and the tram or walk after it as one trip, often typed *In a vehicle*. `splitPlan` (`parser.js`) looks for cuts in the GPS points of such trips (Timeline's generic vehicle, train, tram, metro and bus trips, and guessed ones; never activities, simulated trips, flights or trips an activity replaced):

- **A stop:** points within 350 m of each other for 5 minutes or more, or a gap of 5 minutes or more between two points that only moved at walking pace (under 15 km/h, and a third of the speed on either side) while the trip around it went much faster.
- **A change of speed:** a stretch at 30 km/h or more and 2 km long next to one under 15 km/h for 5 minutes, after runs shorter than 3 minutes are taken into their neighbours, the slow one under 1/2.5 of the fast one.

Each leg must be 250 m and 2 minutes or more. A cut is *sure* when the speed on either side differs 2.5 times or more at a stop, or (with a station pack) when it is at a station, or a change of speed at a station after a stretch along the railway; a stop away from any station with the same speed on both sides (under 1.6 times) is dropped unless it lasted 15 minutes. `splitTrips` (`split.js`) runs in `setData` on D only, after Strava is laid over: in *Automatic* (Settings → *Trips Timeline merged*, `PREFS.split`) the sure cuts are made and trips with only unsure ones are marked *Split?* (`D.offer`), *Only suggest* marks them all, *Off* leaves everything as loaded (D is then the same as before this existed). Each leg is a trip of its own (`D.leg`: leg number, the Timeline trip it came from, the stop after it), so filters, routines, Insights, playback and the map see the legs. The trip's distance is shared by the legs by the length of their trails, so totals don't change; the time stopped is no longer counted as travelling. A leg's type comes from the lines it follows in a station pack (train, tram or metro, `BY_PACK`), else the longest leg keeps a type Timeline gave, else it is guessed like any untyped trip (`retypeGuessed(R, only)`). A stop at a station ends the leg before at the station and starts the next one there. *Keep as one trip* and *Split* in the trip details are kept per trip by its start time (`PREFS.splitKeep`, `PREFS.splitYes`); a trip a MyRules correction points at is only offered. On a real ten-year Timeline (25,600 trips) Automatic split 427 trips without station packs and marked 458 more; total distance stayed the same to the kilometre.

## Map snaps

`snaps.js`. A map snap draws your trips between two stations along one line of a station pack, to clean up their paths. Views and layers → *New map snap* (it needs a pack): tap the two ends on the map (`snapTap`: the nearest station or stop within 1 km, a railway station winning over a tram or metro stop up to 300 m nearer), then the panel offers the types whose lines join both (train, tram or metro, one type the whole way) and each way along them (`packLines`), and shows your trips between the two ends: solid when they would take the line, dashed when not, with *Trips that start and end within* (100 m–2 km) and *Follow the line for at least* (the share of GPS points within 500 m, as for routines). The rest of the map's layers are off while it's open. Saved in MyRules under `snaps` (schema `map_snap`): mode, the two ends with their names, `radius_m`, the line as a polyline, `match` and `other_types`; both ways, and no period, days or times (routines have those). `mrApplySnaps` runs after the routines and before the corrections: a trip no routine took, between the two ends either way, whose type is the snap's, missing, Vehicle or guessed (any type with *Other types too*), that follows the line for `match` % or more takes the stretch of the line between its ends (`mrSnapTrip`, as for a routine's line) and the snap's type. With several snaps, the one it follows most closely. MyRules lists them under *Map snaps* (edit, switch off, delete); a snapped trip's details say which one drew it.

## Station packs

A pack is a country's railway stations, tram and metro stops and its railway, tram, metro and light rail lines (no sidings, yards or freight-only lines), simplified to 12 m, built from OpenStreetMap by `source/packs/build_pack.py` (format in its docstring). `.github/workflows/station-packs.yml` builds every country in `source/packs/countries.json` from Geofabrik extracts (openstreetmap.fr as a fallback) every three months, on demand (with a list of countries), and when `source/packs/` changes on main, and force-pushes them as one commit to the `packs` branch, so the repository's history doesn't grow. The app fetches `index.json` and `<CC>.json.gz` from `raw.githubusercontent.com` (GitHub's release downloads can't be fetched by a web page), only when you choose a country: after an import (`packOffer`, the countries your trips are in, ticked when they hold a twentieth of them; each offered once, `PREFS.packAsked`) or in Settings → *Station packs*. Packs are kept in IndexedDB (`btdt-packs`) and loaded before the trips. They name and place the stops of split trips, type their legs, and give routines a line along the railway offline (`packLines`: the ways along the lines of the routine's type between the track points within 400 m of its ends, or up to 2 km when none are nearer, so a terminus whose lines end on separate platforms is reached from either side). It gives the shortest way and up to three others, found by making the ~150 m cells of the ways so far 4 times as costly and searching again; another way is kept when at most 60% of it runs through cells of one before and it is at most 1.6 times as long. Each is named after up to two stations only it passes, junctions first (*via Haarlem and Hillegom*), to pick in the routine's panel or with its arrows. Dead ends are joined to the nearest track within 50 m, since packs used to leave out the crossovers that join lines at many stations; packs now keep crossovers (`service=crossover`). With a pack for the country of either end, routines never go online for train, tram or metro lines; OpenStreetMap's live lookup (Overpass) is used only where there is no pack, and for buses and ferries. While a routine's ghost trips are on the main map, its other layers (paths, pins, heatmap, squares, countries, beacons) are taken off (`layerToggles`) and put back when the panel closes. Adding or removing a pack splits the trips again, keeping the filters (`refreshData`).

They also name the stops in Regular places (`spotStop`, `packs.js`; airports first, see below): a spot whose stays last 30 minutes or less on average, with a railway station within 200 m of the middle of its stays or a tram, metro or light rail stop within 80 m, is that stop. It takes the station's name when Takeout gave none, a train, tram or metro icon in teal on its pin and in lists, the kind (*Railway station*, *Tram stop*…) in *Where you spent time*, and counts as a *Transport stop* in *Time by kind of place* (and toward the 5 places it needs). Its page says where the name came from, and *Name this place* starts with the name and the Transport stop kind. A MyRules rule, Home and Near home always win. It is worked out once per spot for the data shown, from all its stays (`D.stopBy`, rebuilt with the spot keys). On a real ten-year Timeline with the Dutch pack, 188 of 4,024 spots became stops (Amsterdam Centraal, Leiden Centraal, De Wittenkade…).

Airports are named the same way without any pack, from the airports built into the app (`airportAt`): a spot whose stays last 6 hours or less on average, with the middle of its stays within 2 km of a large airport's point, 1.5 km of a medium one or 1 km of a small one, is "<city> Airport (<code>)", an *Airport* (`role: 'airport'`, plane icon), checked before stations. On the same Timeline, 87 spots became airports (Bogotá, Toronto, Basel-Mulhouse, Bucharest Otopeni…).

## Bad GPS jumps

A corrupted GPS fix (a digit dropped from the longitude, or a stale position such as home showing up mid-trip) puts a point kilometres off the route, which draws a spike on the map and inflates the distance. Such a point is left out of the trip's line when reaching it means moving impossibly fast for the trip's transport (one and a half times its fastest believable average speed, e.g. 22 km/h on foot and 75 km/h by bike, and 160 km/h on the road) and either the line goes out to it and comes straight back, or it is the trip's start or end and the rest of the trip is consistent (and the trip couldn't have gone between its two ends in its time, or the end repeats a point from inside the trip, or the trip or stay right before or after it, within 5 minutes, is where the line is next to the end and not at the end). A regular train (one whose line never keeps up over 200 km/h, unlike a Thalys, Eurostar or the HSL) takes 200 km/h for a point its line goes out to and straight back from. Jumps under 1 km are left alone, as is a jump under 5 km at an end with under a minute to the next point. A distance from Timeline or along the GPS trail that counted the detour loses it (Timeline's figure is shown in the trip details), and a straight-line distance is redrawn between the new ends. These trips say so in their details and carry the flag *bad GPS points left out*. A trip with only its two ends has no line to check, so its ends are checked against the stays and trips around it: every trip end and stay, in time order, makes a track of where you were (ends and stays within 1 km in a row are one stop). A stop holding one end of such a trip is a bad position when it lasted under 6 hours, is at least 5 km from the stops on both sides while those are close together (out and straight back), and is reached or left at over 300 km/h (within a day of a flight, faster than a plane, so a layover Timeline missed a flight for stays). Its trip ends and stays move to the stop next to it in time, and those trips' lines and distances follow; a trip whose type was guessed from its speed is guessed again. Timeline also sometimes puts a stay at the wrong place (a place it matched wrongly, km away) and the ends of the trips on either side of it there too, while those trips' own trails say where you were: the trail before runs out to the stay and the trail after comes straight back. When both trips have lines and the stay lies at least 1 km from the trail points next to it, those points are close together and the trails go on from them at a believable speed, and the stay is reached or left at over 300 km/h (both legs too fast for their trip), the stay and both trip ends move to the trail point nearer in time. A Timeline distance that was only the straight line to the wrong place is then measured along the trail. Strava activities, simulated trips and flights are left as they are. Trips saved on the device before this check existed are cleaned when the app opens.

A trip with no transport type from Timeline (or one that didn't fit its speed) gets a guessed one, worked out again each time the app opens (`retypeGuessed`). First the speed: under 7 km/h is walking, under 20 km/h cycling, and anything up to 300 km/h a car, so the GPS jump check above treats it as road traffic (trips made from GPS points use the speed they kept while moving, see below). The speed along the way then checks that (`profileMode`, on trips with 4 or more points): a walk or ride with 1.5 km, and a quarter of its distance, on stretches over 40 km/h was in a vehicle, and a drive averaging under 30 km/h that never went over 26 km/h on any of three or more stretches of 200 m is a bike ride. A car guess that still averages over 160 km/h once cleaned is a train. Next come your own trips between the same two places (`TripIndex`: both ends within 400 m, either way round, and an average speed within 6 km/h or 35% of the trip's): when at least 3 of them, 60% of them on one kind of transport (a taxi counts as a car), agree and the trip's speed fits that kind, it takes the type most of them have. On a real ten-year Timeline (23,000 typed trips), hiding Google's type on one trip in five and guessing it agreed with Google 84% of the time, up from 70% with speed alone (tram 0% to 68%, car 66% to 86%, train 52% to 75%); on the trips since 2020, whose types are better kept, 90% up from 76%. An untyped trip whose distance is far more than the line between its ends is measured along its GPS trail when it has one, not the straight line, so a ride that comes back to where it started isn't a few metres long. Each guess keeps how it was made and how sure it is (`R.gby`, `R.gconf`; shown in the trip details). A car guess whose line mostly follows (within about 150 to 300 m) the lines of your trips that Timeline did type, all of one motorised kind, takes that kind, so the commute Timeline typed as a train on other days is a train on the days it didn't (60% of the line covered, and at least 15 percentage points more than any other kind; not when the trip is too fast for that kind). Otherwise, a car guess that leaves from and arrives at stays named like a railway station (Station, Centraal, Hbf, Bahnhof, Gare, Estación and so on; not gas, police, bus or metro stations) is a train, and so is one with a station at one end that spent most of its distance at a steady 50 km/h or more. Strava activities and guessed flights are left as they are.

After MyRules (`mrLearn`), guessed trips vote again with your own fixes in: a type you set or checked counts three times, a routine's or a recorded activity's twice. A guess that changes says so in its details. Trips worth a look are marked *Check* (`D.chk`, with the suggested type): guesses under 50% sure; a type from your files that 5 or more of your like trips between the same places disagree with (80% of them on another kind, and no more than 10% sharing its own), among cycling and motorised types; and a ride from your files averaging over 32 km/h. *Check N trips whose type may be wrong* in Filters shows only those, over all time. A checked trip's details ask *Is this the right type?*: keeping it writes a correction with the type (for a guess) or `"checked": true` (for a type from your files), changing it writes the new type, and the next trip to check opens. Trips you fixed or checked, recorded activities and flights are never offered.

## Places Google moved

Timeline saves a stay as a place and that place's location today, not where it was when you were there. When a business moves (an employer relocating across town after you left), every old stay there moves with it, along with the ends of the trips to and from them. The GPS points recorded during each stay show where you really were, so a place is put back where they say when that is clear:

- A stay's GPS spot is the middle of the points recorded during it, when none of them is within 2 km of the place and most are within 500 m of that middle. A stay with no points of its own uses the last point before it and the first after it (each within 10 minutes, and within 500 m of each other).
- A place is moved when at least 3 of its stays have a GPS spot over 2 km from it, within 500 m of each other. Those stays take the middle of their GPS spots, and trips ending or starting there within 10 minutes take it too, with their distance measured along their trail between the new ends. Those trips carry the flag *bad GPS points left out*.
- Stays whose GPS agrees with the place (later visits to the new building) are left where they are, as is a place that is off only once. Nothing about anyone's places is built into the app.

This happens when a file is read, so load Timeline.json again to apply it to data saved before. It covers the on-device Timeline.json from Android and iOS.

## Trips that disagree with their stays

Timeline's trips often disagree with the stays on either side of them, and the stays are right. A stay is a trip's own when it ends (or starts) within 5 minutes of the trip. Three things are fixed:

- **Stale ends.** A trip's first or last point is more than 1 km from its own stay while the next point along the trail is closer to the stay than to that end (a stale position, often home or where the car was earlier). The end moves to the stay.
- **Parked time.** A road trip that starts at least 10 minutes before its trail leaves its stay for good (two points in a row over 1 km away; one point out there is GPS jitter) starts when it leaves, and the stay before it lasts until then. The same goes for a trip that arrives at least 10 minutes before it ends. The parked jitter no longer counts as distance.
- **Straight-line distances.** When Timeline's distance is no more than the straight line between its ends (0.1 km for a 7 km loop), or shorter than the straight line between where the trip really started and ended, the distance is measured along the GPS trail instead, as long as that is drivable in the time. Timeline's figure is shown in the trip details.

These trips carry the flag *bad GPS points left out*, except where only the distance changed. Strava activities, simulated trips, flights and trips with no trail are left as they are.

## Records.json under Timeline

Load the on-device Timeline.json together with Takeout Records.json files (all at once, or one after the other) to get the best of both. Timeline still decides where you stayed, how you travelled and where each trip starts and ends. Records.json, which keeps a GPS point about every minute where Timeline kept one every few minutes, gives those trips their line: each trip is drawn along the Records points recorded during it, and when they cover the whole trip (no gap over 15 minutes) its distance is measured along them too, with Timeline's own figure shown in the trip details. Points less accurate than 200 m are left out, and when two phones recorded at once, a trip uses the phone its own Timeline points came from. On days only Records.json covers, trips are rebuilt from its points as before. On days Timeline covers, a trip rebuilt from Records.json is still kept when it falls in a gap Timeline left (older Timeline data often has a stay and nothing else on a day of driving): at most a fifth of it may overlap Timeline's trips and at most half its stays. Flights always come from Timeline. The Records points are saved on the device with the trips, so files loaded later are matched too.

## Simulated trips

A file in the on-device Timeline.json format with `"simulated": true` at the top is read as simulated trips, for example trips rebuilt from booking emails. Where they clash with an exported Timeline, the simulated trips win: an exported trip that overlaps a simulated one for at least half of its time (or half of the simulated trip's time) is left out, and exported stays are cut back to the time around it. A few minutes' overlap at either end is not a clash, so the walk to the station stays. Everything else on those days still comes from the export.

## Times

Times and dates show the local time where you were. The on-device Timeline.json writes each time with its UTC offset, which is used as it is. Takeout's Semantic Location History, Records.json and Strava write times in UTC only, so those trips and stays take the time zone of the place they started (found from the country borders and time zone data built into the app, daylight saving included). Only when no time zone can be found, such as far out at sea, does a time fall back to this device's time zone. Some older trips in the on-device Timeline.json carry winter time all year round (UTC+1 for Spain in September); when a file's offset is the place's offset in the other season but not on that day, the place's offset is used instead. Those trips can also carry the home time zone while abroad (UTC+1 on a trip in Canada), so an offset two hours or more from the place's is replaced by the place's too, unless it matches where the trip ended.

## Countries

A place's country comes from the built-in borders (Natural Earth, simplified to about 1 km), with these rules everywhere:

- Places just off the simplified coastline, such as a beach or a harbour, take the country with the nearest border within 25 km, unless an airport is closer (for small islands the borders leave out). Further out at sea they take the nearest airport's country.
- Overseas parts the borders fold into another country (Réunion, Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Mayotte, Bonaire, Christmas Island…) are found from the airport data and keep their own code and time zone: a place takes the territory when one of its airports is within 300 km and closer than any of the main country's airports.
- Within 3 km of a border, an airport within 5 km on the other side decides which side a place is on, as the simplified line can put a border town on the wrong side.
- Gibraltar, the Vatican, Ceuta and Melilla, which the borders leave out or give to a neighbour, are outlined by hand.

## Layout

- **Opening view:** the app opens on the period navigator at this week (or the week of your latest data, for an older export). *Navigator* in Filters (where *Last 7 days* used to be) goes back to it. Tapping the navigator's date opens a calendar (days with trips have a dot, the period on show is shaded) with a Today button at the bottom; the top bar itself has no Today button. With *All* or a *Date range* the top bar shows just what's selected and the filter button.
- **Map:** full-screen map with the time slider in a bar at the bottom, centred above the dock. When a single day is chosen, the top bar becomes a navigator: switch between Day, Week (Monday to Sunday), Month and Year, and the arrows (or the arrow keys) step to the previous or next one with trips. The filter button is at the left of the top bar and the Settings gear at its right, in the same place on every tab and screen size. Settings opens as a panel on the right (a sheet from the bottom on a phone). *Imported data*, at the top of Filters, opens in the filters' place: the summary of what is loaded, *Import more files* (location history and activity files alike) and *Remove from this device*, and the list of files loaded, each with what it was read as and its trips; the arrow goes back to Filters. The playback button is bottom-left: it shows or hides the time slider and play controls in their bar just above the dock (the legend, and on a phone the map buttons, move up above it). Views and layers are bottom-right: Mercator, Equal Earth or Globe; paths; a heatmap of where you travelled or stayed, based on GPS points, paths (how many trips went along each route, flights included) or time spent, with dot size and intensity; and countries visited, in blue where your only visit was a layover at the airport (flew in and out within a day, never over 5 km from it).
- **One-finger zoom** (touch screens, every map in the app): tap, then tap again and keep the finger down, and drag down to zoom in or up to zoom out, around the spot tapped, as in Google Maps (`L.Map.TapDragZoom` in `map.js`). It ends on a whole zoom level when the finger lifts. A plain double tap still zooms in one step, and a second finger hands over to the pinch.
- **Speed paths** (Layers on the map): the average speed along each stretch of the map, from every trip in the filters that went along it (flights left out), red under 5 km/h through yellow and green to purple at 120 km/h and over, with thicker lines where more trips went. When every trip in the filters is of one kind, the colours fit it: on foot 1 to 7 km/h (running 6 to 14, walking and running together 2 to 14), cycling 6 to 30, road vehicles 10 to 120, tram and subway 5 to 60, train 20 to 200, and boats 3 to 40. Strava water and snow sports get their own when they are all one kind: paddling, rowing, swimming and surfing 1 to 10, sailing, windsurfing and kitesurfing 4 to 30, Nordic and backcountry skiing and snowshoeing 2 to 20, alpine skiing and snowboarding 5 to 70, ice skating 5 to 35 (snow sports of mixed kinds 3 to 70), and inline skating, skateboarding and roller skiing 5 to 30. Each trip's speed comes from the time between its GPS points, over stretches of at least 20 seconds so a dense Strava recording doesn't turn GPS jitter into speed; a trip with only its two ends counts at its average speed. The lines are followed through a grid of 10-pixel cells at the map's zoom, and each step from one cell to the next adds that trip's speed to the link between them, drawn between the middles of the points in each cell. Only trips near the view are gathered, again after zooming or panning away. Speed paths and Paths switch each other off; a line can still be tapped for its trips. It shows on the Mercator view.
- **Heart rate** (any activity whose file recorded it: Strava, or a GPX, TCX or FIT file). Zones are shares of your maximum heart rate: Z1 Recovery under 60%, Z2 Endurance 60–70%, Z3 Tempo 70–80%, Z4 Threshold 80–90%, Z5 Maximum 90% and over. The maximum is the one set in Settings → Heart rate (100–230 bpm), or else the highest any activity held, taking each activity's 99th percentile so a strap's brief spike doesn't count. *Heart rate paths* (Layers) draw the activities with heart rate the way speed paths draw speed (same 20-second stretches, cells and thickness), coloured by the zone of the average heart rate along each stretch; trips without heart rate are left out and can't be tapped there. With it on, the open activity's own line is coloured by zone too, as is the heart rate line in its chart, and the chart's readout names the zone. The trip details show the average and highest heart rate and the time in each zone; a sample counts until the next one, but no more than twice the usual gap, so a paused recording isn't counted. The *Heart rate* card in Insights shows the time in each zone over the activities in the filters, and a fitness trend per sport (Strava sport, or cycling, running and walking by transport type; e-bikes left out): the usual speed is the median while moving (over 2 km/h), rounded to whole km/h or mph from 15 up and to halves below, and each month's value is the median heart rate of the moments within 8% of that speed, after each activity's first 5 minutes, with at least 24 such samples. A straight line fitted through the months (weighted by the square root of the activities in each) gives the change shown under the chart.
- **Explorer squares** (Layers on the map): the world cut into zoom 14 map tiles (about 1.5 km across in the Netherlands), and every square a trip on the ground passed through in the filters filled in. Each line is followed a quarter of a square at a time; a straight stretch of over 30 squares between two points (a long gap in the GPS, or a trip with only its ends) only counts its ends. The biggest cluster (the largest connected block of squares whose four neighbours are all visited) is darker, and the max square (the biggest all-visited n × n block) is outlined. Tapping or hovering over a square says when you first got there and how many trips passed through. Insights has an *Explorer squares* card with the count, cluster, max square and new squares each year. Shows on the Mercator view.
- **Filters:** Years and Months only show for *All*, and Days of the week for *All* and *Date range*; the other choices already say which days, so those filters are hidden and set back to everything there. Transport is a tree of kinds (Road, Flights, Public transport, On foot, Cycling, Water, Other): the arrow opens a kind to show its types (Car, Taxi, Motorbike…), and Strava sports sit under their type. Ticking a kind ticks everything under it, and a kind with only some of its types on shows as partly on.
- **Loading screen:** while files are read (and the demo, saved trips or an export are opened), a full-screen loader shows a donut that fills with the reading progress, or spins when there is no percentage, around an icon that changes about once a second between transport types, a location pin and world landmarks (castle, temples, mosque, church, synagogue, stadium, Ferris wheel, fort; filled Material Icons). The ring and the disc behind the icon take on each icon's colour.
- **Card explanations:** a card's explanation (Countries over time, Top routes…) is behind the (i) next to its title.
- **Insights:** the same navigator as the map, in the same place (the period is shared, so stepping on any tab moves them all, and it stays put as you switch tabs), then summary, how you travel, weekly rhythm, change over time, top routes, places, records, countries over time (a strip you can scroll and zoom, one colour per country; days in two countries are split once zoomed in far enough, and stops of under a day are marked: a true layover, where you flew in and out and stayed within 5 km of the airport, is a circle below the strip (shown once the axis names the months), and a stop where you got out, a layover with a visit or a short stop on the ground, is a downward pentagon above it; stretches with no records are drawn faded in the country you were in before and after, or half each when those differ, and are left out of the day counts; it only shows with every kind of transport selected, and otherwise offers a button to show all transport), countries and CO₂.
- **Timeline:** the same navigator, then every trip in the filter range, newest first. Tap a trip to see it on a map at the top of the list. The search box under the navigator narrows the list to trips in the filters whose title (from Strava, an activity file or *Edit trip*), start and end places, or type hold every word typed, ignoring case and accents (`tlMatch`); the day headers and the end of the list count only those. Escape or × clears it, and a trip opened from the map or Insights that the search hides clears it too.

## Place pages

In Insights, tap a place under *Where you spent time* to open its page, covering your whole history rather than the filters: the same figures as a pin's page (see below), and the spots inside it. A place in that list is everything with the same name (often a town). The spots inside it are told apart by the place ID Google gives each stay in Timeline.json and Takeout, so two shops on one street stay separate; stays without one are grouped as described under *Regular places on the map*. The map, routes and the Places list itself still group by distance as before. Each spot has a *Google Maps* link (`google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=lat,lng&query_place_id=…`) to see what it is: nothing is looked up by the app, and only the spot you tap is sent to Google. Place IDs also appear in a stay's raw data in the JSON export.

### Regular places on the map

*Regular places* in the map's layers puts a pin on each spot you stayed at within the filters and have stayed at at least 3 times over your whole history (up to 400 pins, the most visited first; home is green). Spots are the same as on the place pages: Google's place ID. A stay without a place ID joins the nearest place ID within 100 m (about how far off a GPS position can be in a city), so one place doesn't show as two pins; stays left over group with each other within 50 m. Google sometimes gives a place a new place ID and your history keeps both: two place IDs within 25 m, each with at least 3 visits, count as one place when your visits moved from one to the other (in time order, at most a tenth of the smaller one's visits fall on the wrong side of the handover), while neighbouring shops, whose visits are mixed in time, stay apart. A spot page lists nearby spots (within 150 m) with a *Merge into this* button for what that misses; merges are saved on the device (in the app's preferences) and *Undo the merges you made* reverses them. Google's own home and work tags are not used anywhere in BT•DT, as they often don't match what you labelled: home is where most nights are spent, only the spot where you spent most time there is *Home*, and other spots within 300 m of it are *Near home*. Tapping a pin opens that spot's page. A switch at its top picks *All history* or *These filters* (remembered; the page follows the filters and the period navigator while it's open): visits, how often (a week, a month, or every few months), time there, average, typical (median) and longest stay, first and last visit, a weekday-by-hour grid of when you're there, and every visit (the latest 20 first, then *Show all*). When the town has other spots, *All of …* opens the town's page, whose spots each open their own page. While a place page is open, its pins (the spot, or every spot of a town) turn red, grow and come to the front, and the other pins fade. Pins show on the Mercator view, so turning them on switches to it. Zoomed out (below zoom 15), pins closer than 56 px are grouped into one round marker with how many places it holds (`renderPins`: most visited first, each joining the first group near it, the marker at its busiest place; home's colour and icon when home is in it, teal when it holds named places). Tapping it zooms in on its places. Only pins in view (plus a quarter around) are drawn, again after each move, and the pins of an open place page always show on their own.

## Export

At the bottom of Filters, *Export* downloads what the filters currently show (every filter applies: days, years, months, days of the week, time of day, transport, guessed types, source and countries; the map's time slider doesn't narrow it).

- **Raw data (JSON):** the filters in words, then every matching trip, newest first, in the same format as a trip's *{ } Raw data* (GPS points included), then the matching stays (times, place name, place ID, country and position).
- **Table (CSV):** one row per trip, without the GPS points: UTC and local times, duration, type, source, distance, route, countries, start and end positions, CO₂, number of GPS points and flags. It opens in Excel or Numbers.

## Rebuild

Run `python3 build.py`. It assembles `dist/index.html` from `src/` and copies `static/` (the manifest, service worker and icons).

The app icons in `static/` come from the BTDT Icons frames in Figma: `icons/icon-source.png` (the home-screen icon, a full-bleed square) and `icons/favicon.svg` (the browser-tab pin, drawn at 512 px as `icons/favicon-source.png`), made into every size by `python3 icons/make_icons.py`. Data saved on a device under the old name, Timeliner, is picked up and copied to the new storage on first open.

## Date and time format

Settings → *Date and time* sets how dates (day month year `17 Nov 2026`, month day year `Nov 17, 2026`, or year-month-day `2026-11-17`) and times (24-hour `14:05` or 12-hour `2:05 PM`) are shown. *Automatic*, the default, follows the device's language and region (`LOCALE_FMT`, read from `navigator.languages` with `Intl.DateTimeFormat`). The choice is saved with the other preferences (`PREFS.dateF`, `PREFS.timeF`) and applies everywhere through `fmtHM`, `fmtHour`, `fmtDM`, `fmtDMY` and `fmtRange` in `core.js` (`niceDate`, `longDate`, `periodLabel`, `clock` and `localTime` use them). Distance units (Settings → *Units*) work the same way: *Automatic*, the default, is miles where the device's region is the US, the UK, Liberia or Myanmar and kilometres elsewhere (`LOCALE_FMT.unit`); Kilometres or Miles override it (`PREFS.unitF`, resolved into `PREFS.unit`). A km saved before this counts as Automatic, a saved mi stays. Month and day names stay in English; date fields in the filters and MyRules dialogs are the browser's own and follow its locale. Exports and MyRules files keep ISO dates and 24-hour times.
