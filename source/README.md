# BT•DT (Been There · Done That)

BT•DT 2.0 shows where you've been and how you get around, using your Google Timeline. It is the successor to Transport Footprint, which is frozen as v1.0 in `../versions/v1.0/`.

## Open it

- **On a Mac or PC:** open `dist/index.html` in a browser.
- **On a phone:** host the `dist/` folder over HTTPS (for example GitHub Pages or Netlify), open it, and choose *Add to Home screen*. It then starts offline, and your last import stays saved on the phone.

Your Timeline file is read on the device and is never uploaded.

## Strava

In Settings, *Load Strava export* takes the ZIP from Strava (Settings → My Account → Download or Delete Your Account), or single .gpx, .fit or .tcx files. Only the activity files inside the ZIP are read, so a large export with photos is fine. A Strava activity replaces every Timeline trip that is at least half inside its time (whatever transport Timeline guessed), and activities Timeline missed are added. The replaced Timeline trips are only hidden while Strava is shown: choosing Timeline as the only source in Filters brings them back. Virtual rides are skipped. The Strava activities are saved apart from the Timeline trips, so re-importing either one keeps the match. Each Strava trip keeps its sport (Kayaking, Mountain bike, Trail run…) under the matching transport type, which is what the trip is called in the app; the Transport filter lists the sports present under their type. Data loaded before sports were read shows them after loading the Strava export again.

An activity's name from Strava is the trip's title (in the Timeline list and the trip details), with its sport under it, and its description is shown in the trip details. The gear it was recorded with (activities.csv's *Activity Gear*) is listed in the trip details, and in Filters each bike or pair of shoes is a chip under the transport type it was mostly used for (bikes under Cycling, below its Strava sports), with *No gear* for that type's other trips; ticking or unticking the type does its gear too. Ticking a gear on also ticks the Strava sports (or types) its trips are under, so its trips show even after *None*, leaving the type partly on; unticking it unticks those that no other ticked gear needs. Ticking a sport on when none of the gear its trips use is ticked ticks that gear. During playback, a ride on a Halfbike or cargo bike moves with that bike's icon. The export's bikes.csv and shoes.csv say which gear is a bike and which are shoes; a bike whose name, brand or model says Halfbike (or Kolelinia) gets the Halfbike icon, and one that names a front-loading cargo bike (Urban Arrow, Babboe, Bullitt, bakfiets, cargo…) the cargo bike icon, also on its trips. Without those files, gear used mostly for cycling is a bike and gear used on foot is shoes. Insights has a *Your gear* card with the distance, activities and dates of each gear in the trips shown; tapping one filters to its trips. Data loaded before names and gear were kept shows them from the activities.csv columns it kept, or after loading the export again.

## Dawarich

[Dawarich](https://dawarich.app) is a self-hosted location history app. Its exports load with the same *Load Timeline file* button, and are recognised by what is inside them:

- **Export as GeoJSON or GPX** (Points page): a ZIP holding one file, GeoJSON with a point feature per GPS point (time in `properties.timestamp`) or GPX written by Dawarich (one track with every point). The file unzipped works too.
- **Export my data** (Settings): a ZIP with `manifest.json` and the points as JSON Lines in `points/YYYY/YYYY-MM.jsonl`, plus `visits/` and `tracks/`. A month of points unzipped (`.jsonl`) loads on its own too.

Dawarich keeps raw GPS points, so trips and stays are worked out from them, all read as a stream so a large export never has to fit in memory as text. Points less accurate than 200 m are left out. A stay is points that keep within 150 m of their middle for at least 10 minutes (a phone that stops reporting while still counts, when its next point is there too); stays at the same spot less than 15 minutes apart are one. The points between two stays are a trip when they get 500 m from where they started or end 200 m from it, starting and ending at the stays' middles; a run ends at a gap of over 30 minutes, and a jump of over 150 km at over 180 km/h is a flight, as in Records.json. The transport is the one on Dawarich's track segments when the account export has them (low-confidence ones are ignored), and a trip is split where it changes; otherwise it is guessed from the speed, as for loose Timeline points, and marked as guessed. A stay takes the name of the Dawarich visit covering most of its time (or most of the visit's) within 500 m, and that visit's place position when it is within 300 m; declined and deleted visits are left out. Times are UTC in Dawarich, so they take the time zone of the place, like Records.json.

Loaded together with a Google Timeline, Dawarich works like Records.json: Timeline wins the days it covers, Dawarich trips are kept in the gaps it left, and Timeline trips are drawn along Dawarich's GPS points.

## Trips from Timeline's loose GPS points

Older on-device Timeline data often keeps GPS points while you were moving but no trip for them, so a day of driving shows up as a few stays and nothing else. Points outside every trip and stay are joined into trips (a run ends at a gap of over 30 minutes, a jump of over 50 km, or a trip or stay) when they get at least 500 m from where they started. Their transport type is guessed from the speed they kept while moving, weighted by distance (the speed at or above which half the distance was covered, and never less than the trip's overall average, so one long fast stretch between a few slow legs isn't a bike ride): 30 km/h or more is a car, 10 km/h or more a bike, slower is walking. They are marked as guessed, and the trip details say they were made from loose points.

## Bad GPS jumps

A corrupted GPS fix (a digit dropped from the longitude, or a stale position such as home showing up mid-trip) puts a point kilometres off the route, which draws a spike on the map and inflates the distance. Such a point is left out of the trip's line when reaching it means moving impossibly fast for the trip's transport (one and a half times its fastest believable average speed, e.g. 22 km/h on foot and 75 km/h by bike, and 160 km/h on the road) and either the line goes out to it and comes straight back, or it is the trip's start or end and the rest of the trip is consistent (and the trip couldn't have gone between its two ends in its time, or the end repeats a point from inside the trip). Jumps under 1 km are left alone, as is a jump under 5 km at an end with under a minute to the next point. A distance from Timeline or along the GPS trail that counted the detour loses it (Timeline's figure is shown in the trip details), and a straight-line distance is redrawn between the new ends. These trips say so in their details and carry the flag *bad GPS points left out*. A trip with only its two ends has no line to check, so its ends are checked against the stays and trips around it: every trip end and stay, in time order, makes a track of where you were (ends and stays within 1 km in a row are one stop). A stop holding one end of such a trip is a bad position when it lasted under 6 hours, is at least 5 km from the stops on both sides while those are close together (out and straight back), and is reached or left at over 300 km/h (within a day of a flight, faster than a plane, so a layover Timeline missed a flight for stays). Its trip ends and stays move to the stop next to it in time, and those trips' lines and distances follow; a trip whose type was guessed from its speed is guessed again. Strava activities, simulated trips and flights are left as they are. Trips saved on the device before this check existed are cleaned when the app opens.

A trip with no transport type from Timeline (or one that didn't fit its speed) gets a guessed one, worked out again each time the app opens. First the speed: under 7 km/h is walking, under 25 km/h cycling, and anything up to 300 km/h a car, so the GPS jump check above treats it as road traffic (trips made from GPS points use the speed they kept while moving, see below). A car guess that still averages over 160 km/h once cleaned is a train. A car guess whose line mostly follows (within about 150 to 300 m) the lines of your trips that Timeline did type, all of one motorised kind, takes that kind, so the commute Timeline typed as a train on other days is a train on the days it didn't (60% of the line covered, and at least 15 percentage points more than any other kind; not when the trip is too fast for that kind). Otherwise, a car guess that leaves from and arrives at stays named like a railway station (Station, Centraal, Hbf, Bahnhof, Gare, Estación and so on; not gas, police, bus or metro stations) is a train, and so is one with a station at one end that spent most of its distance at a steady 50 km/h or more. Strava activities and guessed flights are left as they are.

## Places Google moved

Timeline saves a stay as a place and that place's location today, not where it was when you were there. When a business moves (an employer relocating across town after you left), every old stay there moves with it, along with the ends of the trips to and from them. The GPS points recorded during each stay show where you really were, so a place is put back where they say when that is clear:

- A stay's GPS spot is the middle of the points recorded during it, when none of them is within 2 km of the place and most are within 500 m of that middle. A stay with no points of its own uses the last point before it and the first after it (each within 10 minutes, and within 500 m of each other).
- A place is moved when at least 3 of its stays have a GPS spot over 2 km from it, within 500 m of each other. Those stays take the middle of their GPS spots, and trips ending or starting there within 10 minutes take it too, with their distance measured along their trail between the new ends. Those trips carry the flag *bad GPS points left out*.
- Stays whose GPS agrees with the place (later visits to the new building) are left where they are, as is a place that is off only once. Nothing about anyone's places is built into the app.

This happens when a file is read, so load Timeline.json again to apply it to data saved before. It covers the on-device Timeline.json from Android and iOS.

## Trips that disagree with their stays

Timeline's trips often disagree with the stays on either side of them, and the stays are right. A stay is a trip's own when it ends (or starts) within 5 minutes of the trip. Three things are fixed:

- **Stale ends.** A trip's first or last point is more than 1 km from its own stay while the next point along the trail is closer to the stay than to that end (a stale position, often home or where the car was earlier). The end moves to the stay.
- **Parked time.** A road trip that starts at least 10 minutes before its trail leaves its stay for good (two points in a row over 1 km away; one point out there is GPS jitter) starts when it leaves, and the stay before it lasts until then. The same goes for a trip that arrives at least 10 minutes before it ends. The parked jitter no longer counts as distance.
- **Straight-line distances.** When Timeline's distance is no more than the straight line between its ends (0.1 km for a 7 km loop), or shorter than the straight line between where the trip really started and ended, the distance is measured along the GPS trail instead, as long as that is drivable in the time. Timeline's figure is shown in the trip details.

These trips carry the flag *bad GPS points left out*, except where only the distance changed. Strava activities, simulated trips, flights and trips with no trail are left as they are.

## Records.json under Timeline

Load the on-device Timeline.json together with Takeout Records.json files (all at once, or one after the other) to get the best of both. Timeline still decides where you stayed, how you travelled and where each trip starts and ends. Records.json, which keeps a GPS point about every minute where Timeline kept one every few minutes, gives those trips their line: each trip is drawn along the Records points recorded during it, and when they cover the whole trip (no gap over 15 minutes) its distance is measured along them too, with Timeline's own figure shown in the trip details. Points less accurate than 200 m are left out, and when two phones recorded at once, a trip uses the phone its own Timeline points came from. On days only Records.json covers, trips are rebuilt from its points as before. On days Timeline covers, a trip rebuilt from Records.json is still kept when it falls in a gap Timeline left (older Timeline data often has a stay and nothing else on a day of driving): at most a fifth of it may overlap Timeline's trips and at most half its stays. Flights always come from Timeline. The Records points are saved on the device with the trips, so files loaded later are matched too.

## Simulated trips

A file in the on-device Timeline.json format with `"simulated": true` at the top is read as simulated trips, for example trips rebuilt from booking emails. Where they clash with an exported Timeline, the simulated trips win: an exported trip that overlaps a simulated one for at least half of its time (or half of the simulated trip's time) is left out, and exported stays are cut back to the time around it. A few minutes' overlap at either end is not a clash, so the walk to the station stays. Everything else on those days still comes from the export.

## Times

Times and dates show the local time where you were. The on-device Timeline.json writes each time with its UTC offset, which is used as it is. Takeout's Semantic Location History, Records.json and Strava write times in UTC only, so those trips and stays take the time zone of the place they started (found from the country borders and time zone data built into the app, daylight saving included). Only when no time zone can be found, such as far out at sea, does a time fall back to this device's time zone. Some older trips in the on-device Timeline.json carry winter time all year round (UTC+1 for Spain in September); when a file's offset is the place's offset in the other season but not on that day, the place's offset is used instead. Those trips can also carry the home time zone while abroad (UTC+1 on a trip in Canada), so an offset two hours or more from the place's is replaced by the place's too, unless it matches where the trip ended.

## Countries

A place's country comes from the built-in borders (Natural Earth, simplified to about 1 km), with these rules everywhere:

- Places just off the simplified coastline, such as a beach or a harbour, take the country with the nearest border within 25 km, unless an airport is closer (for small islands the borders leave out). Further out at sea they take the nearest airport's country.
- Overseas parts the borders fold into another country (Réunion, Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Mayotte, Bonaire, Christmas Island…) are found from the airport data and keep their own code and time zone: a place takes the territory when one of its airports is within 300 km and closer than any of the main country's airports.
- Within 3 km of a border, an airport within 5 km on the other side decides which side a place is on, as the simplified line can put a border town on the wrong side.
- Gibraltar, the Vatican, Ceuta and Melilla, which the borders leave out or give to a neighbour, are outlined by hand.

## Layout

- **Opening view:** the app opens on the period navigator at this week (or the week of your latest data, for an older export). *Navigator* in Filters (where *Last 7 days* used to be) goes back to it. Tapping the navigator's date opens a calendar (days with trips have a dot, the period on show is shaded) with a Today button at the bottom; the top bar itself has no Today button. With *All* or a *Date range* the top bar shows just what's selected and the filter button.
- **Map:** full-screen map with the time slider at the top. When a single day is chosen, the top bar becomes a navigator: switch between Day, Week (Monday to Sunday), Month and Year, and the arrows (or the arrow keys) step to the previous or next one with trips. The filter button is at the right of the top bar, in the same place on every tab (the gear in Filters opens Settings). The playback button is bottom-left: it shows or hides the time slider and play controls in the top bar. Views and layers are bottom-right: Mercator, Equal Earth or Globe; paths; a heatmap of where you travelled or stayed, based on GPS points, paths (how many trips went along each route, flights included) or time spent, with dot size and intensity; and countries visited, in blue where your only visit was a layover at the airport (flew in and out within a day, never over 5 km from it).
- **Speed paths** (Layers on the map): the average speed along each stretch of the map, from every trip in the filters that went along it (flights left out), red under 5 km/h through yellow and green to purple at 120 km/h and over, with thicker lines where more trips went. When every trip in the filters is of one kind, the colours fit it: on foot 1 to 7 km/h (running 6 to 14, walking and running together 2 to 14), cycling 6 to 30, road vehicles 10 to 120, tram and subway 5 to 60, train 20 to 200, and boats 3 to 40. Each trip's speed comes from the time between its GPS points, over stretches of at least 20 seconds so a dense Strava recording doesn't turn GPS jitter into speed; a trip with only its two ends counts at its average speed. The lines are followed through a grid of 10-pixel cells at the map's zoom, and each step from one cell to the next adds that trip's speed to the link between them, drawn between the middles of the points in each cell. Only trips near the view are gathered, again after zooming or panning away. Speed paths and Paths switch each other off; a line can still be tapped for its trips. It shows on the Mercator view.
- **Explorer squares** (Layers on the map): the world cut into zoom 14 map tiles (about 1.5 km across in the Netherlands), and every square a trip on the ground passed through in the filters filled in. Each line is followed a quarter of a square at a time; a straight stretch of over 30 squares between two points (a long gap in the GPS, or a trip with only its ends) only counts its ends. The biggest cluster (the largest connected block of squares whose four neighbours are all visited) is darker, and the max square (the biggest all-visited n × n block) is outlined. Tapping or hovering over a square says when you first got there and how many trips passed through. Insights has an *Explorer squares* card with the count, cluster, max square and new squares each year. Shows on the Mercator view.
- **Filters:** Years and Months only show for *All*, and Days of the week for *All* and *Date range*; the other choices already say which days, so those filters are hidden and set back to everything there. Transport is a tree of kinds (Road, Flights, Public transport, On foot, Cycling, Water, Other): the arrow opens a kind to show its types (Car, Taxi, Motorbike…), and Strava sports sit under their type. Ticking a kind ticks everything under it, and a kind with only some of its types on shows as partly on.
- **Card explanations:** a card's explanation (Countries over time, Top routes…) is behind the (i) next to its title.
- **Insights:** the same navigator as the map, in the same place (the period is shared, so stepping on any tab moves them all, and it stays put as you switch tabs), then summary, how you travel, weekly rhythm, change over time, top routes, places, records, countries over time (a strip you can scroll and zoom, one colour per country; days in two countries are split once zoomed in far enough, and stops of under a day are marked: a true layover, where you flew in and out and stayed within 5 km of the airport, is a circle below the strip (shown once the axis names the months), and a stop where you got out, a layover with a visit or a short stop on the ground, is a downward pentagon above it; stretches with no records are drawn faded in the country you were in before and after, or half each when those differ, and are left out of the day counts; it only shows with every kind of transport selected, and otherwise offers a button to show all transport), countries and CO₂.
- **Timeline:** the same navigator, then every trip in the filter range, newest first. Tap a trip to see it on a map at the top of the list.

## Place pages

In Insights, tap a place under *Where you spent time* to open its page, covering your whole history rather than the filters: the same figures as a pin's page (see below), and the spots inside it. A place in that list is everything with the same name (often a town). The spots inside it are told apart by the place ID Google gives each stay in Timeline.json and Takeout, so two shops on one street stay separate; stays without one are grouped as described under *Regular places on the map*. The map, routes and the Places list itself still group by distance as before. Each spot has a *Google Maps* link (`google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=lat,lng&query_place_id=…`) to see what it is: nothing is looked up by the app, and only the spot you tap is sent to Google. Place IDs also appear in a stay's raw data in the JSON export.

### Regular places on the map

*Regular places* in the map's layers puts a pin on each spot you stayed at within the filters and have stayed at at least 3 times over your whole history (up to 400 pins, the most visited first; home is green). Spots are the same as on the place pages: Google's place ID. A stay without a place ID joins the nearest place ID within 100 m (about how far off a GPS position can be in a city), so one place doesn't show as two pins; stays left over group with each other within 50 m. Google sometimes gives a place a new place ID and your history keeps both: two place IDs within 25 m, each with at least 3 visits, count as one place when your visits moved from one to the other (in time order, at most a tenth of the smaller one's visits fall on the wrong side of the handover), while neighbouring shops, whose visits are mixed in time, stay apart. A spot page lists nearby spots (within 150 m) with a *Merge into this* button for what that misses; merges are saved on the device (in the app's preferences) and *Undo the merges you made* reverses them. Google's own home and work tags are not used anywhere in BT•DT, as they often don't match what you labelled: home is where most nights are spent, only the spot where you spent most time there is *Home*, and other spots within 300 m of it are *Near home*. Tapping a pin opens that spot's page. A switch at its top picks *All history* or *These filters* (remembered; the page follows the filters and the period navigator while it's open): visits, how often (a week, a month, or every few months), time there, average, typical (median) and longest stay, first and last visit, a weekday-by-hour grid of when you're there, and every visit (the latest 20 first, then *Show all*). When the town has other spots, *All of …* opens the town's page, whose spots each open their own page. While a place page is open, its pins (the spot, or every spot of a town) turn red, grow and come to the front, and the other pins fade. Pins show on the Mercator view, so turning them on switches to it.

## Export

At the bottom of Filters, *Export* downloads what the filters currently show (every filter applies: days, years, months, days of the week, time of day, transport, guessed types, source and countries; the map's time slider doesn't narrow it).

- **Raw data (JSON):** the filters in words, then every matching trip, newest first, in the same format as a trip's *{ } Raw data* (GPS points included), then the matching stays (times, place name, place ID, country and position).
- **Table (CSV):** one row per trip, without the GPS points: UTC and local times, duration, type, source, distance, route, countries, start and end positions, CO₂, number of GPS points and flags. It opens in Excel or Numbers.

## Rebuild

Run `python3 build.py`. It assembles `dist/index.html` from `src/` and copies `static/` (the manifest, service worker and icons).

The app icons in `static/` come from the BTDT Icons frames in Figma: `icons/icon-source.png` (the home-screen icon, a full-bleed square) and `icons/favicon.svg` (the browser-tab pin, drawn at 512 px as `icons/favicon-source.png`), made into every size by `python3 icons/make_icons.py`. Data saved on a device under the old name, Timeliner, is picked up and copied to the new storage on first open.

## Demo

*Try the demo* on the welcome page opens `demo.json`: three made-up years (13 Nov 2023 to 8 Nov 2026) of someone living in Oud-West, Amsterdam, with an office at the Zuidas. It is mostly cycling, walking and trams on a sketch of Amsterdam's streets, a run in the Vondelpark, the ferry to NDSM, trains to other Randstad cities, a road trip outside the Randstad every couple of months (Texel, Giethoorn, the Ardennes, Hoge Veluwe, Cologne, Zeeland, Maastricht, Groningen, Antwerp, Friesland), and two holidays a year: Vietnam, Peru, South Africa, Japan, Colombia and Tanzania. Four of them change planes on the way (Dubai, Helsinki, Madrid and Doha), so those countries show as layovers only.

`python3 source/demo/make_demo.py` writes it to `static/demo.json` as an on-device Timeline.json, the same every run. It is read by the same parser as your own file but never saved: the demo opens on all its data with Countries visited on (your own setting for that layer is what stays saved), *Exit demo* reloads to the welcome page, and importing your own files replaces it. The service worker caches it the first time it is opened.
